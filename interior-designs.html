A practical way to plan a comfortable interior

Good interior design begins with how a room is used. Before buying furniture or choosing a colour palette, note the activities that happen in the space, the people who use it and the items that need storage. Measure the room, doors, windows and large furniture so that each choice fits the actual space.

Plan movement before decoration

Draw a simple floor sketch and mark door swings, windows and fixed features. Place the largest furniture first, then check that people can walk comfortably between the entrance, seating, storage and other activity zones. Avoid blocking natural light, ventilation openings or access to cupboards. In a small room, a clear route can be more valuable than adding another piece of furniture.

Use lighting in layers

Combine general room lighting with task lighting for reading, cooking or working and softer accent lighting where it is useful. Make use of daylight while considering glare, privacy and heat. Select bulbs with a comfortable colour appearance for the room's purpose, and have electrical work checked by a qualified professional.

Choose a calm, coordinated palette

Start with one or two main colours and repeat a small number of materials or finishes to create visual connection between rooms. Test paint and fabric samples in the actual space at different times of day. Durable, easy-to-clean surfaces are often sensible for kitchens, entrances and busy family areas.
