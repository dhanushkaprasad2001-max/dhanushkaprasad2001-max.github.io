1. Design Overview

The visual presents a small single-storey family home with two bedrooms, a central living area, a compact kitchen, a shared bathroom and a covered entrance porch. The exterior uses light walls, stone-look accents around the porch columns, dark-framed windows and a red tiled hipped roof. The furnished top view suggests a simple layout with sleeping spaces separated by a shared family zone.

The stated 20 × 30-foot rectangle equals 600 square feet. However, the floor-plan dimension arrows indicate 20 × 32 feet, which equals 640 square feet. The final design must choose one accurate external footprint and show whether the porch is included in that measurement. Gross footprint is not the same as net usable indoor area because walls and other construction elements occupy space.

3. Bedroom 1 – Main Bedroom

The image labels the first bedroom as 10 × 12 feet. This size can provide room for a double bed, bedside storage and a wardrobe if the door and window positions are coordinated. Before finalising the furniture layout, draw the bed footprint and wardrobe depth to scale. Check that the bedroom door does not hit the bed or wardrobe and that there is a comfortable route to both sides of the bed where possible.

4. Bedroom 2 – Second Bedroom

The second bedroom is also labelled 10 × 12 feet. Matching bedroom dimensions can simplify furniture planning and allow either room to serve as a child's room, guest room or work-from-home space. The final drawing should identify the window size, sill height, wardrobe position and door swing. Avoid relying on the rendered furniture image to judge actual clearances.

5. Living Room and Family Circulation

The living room is labelled 10 × 14 feet and shown with a compact sofa arrangement. In a small house, the living room also becomes the main circulation zone between the entrance, bedrooms and kitchen. Keep a direct, unobstructed route through the room. Position the television and seating so that doors can open fully and people can pass without squeezing between furniture.

The drawing does not clearly dimension the living-room boundary. Since its labelled length is part of the 38-foot sum of the three left-side rooms, the plan must be redrawn before this size can be relied on.

6. Compact Kitchen

The kitchen is labelled 7 × 10 feet. A compact single-wall or carefully planned L-shaped counter may be suitable, depending on the verified room shape. The construction plan should show the sink, cooktop, refrigerator, preparation counter, storage, electrical outlets and window or exhaust location. Maintain safe working clearance in front of appliances and keep the kitchen entrance clear of dining or living-room furniture.

7. Bathroom and Plumbing

The bathroom is labelled 6 × 7 feet. The concept image shows bathroom fixtures, but it does not provide enough detail to verify the clearances around the toilet, basin and shower. A revised plan should mark the exact fixtures, door opening direction, shower screen, floor fall, floor trap and ventilation opening. Waterproofing details should be specified before tiling.

Coordinate the bathroom and kitchen plumbing early. Keep pipe routes accessible for maintenance and have drainage falls and connection levels checked by a qualified professional. Do not cut structural beams or columns to accommodate plumbing.

8. Front Porch and Entrance

The porch is labelled 5 × 8 feet and is shown as a small sheltered entrance. It can provide a transition between the garden and the living room, but the usable space depends on the final column positions, steps and door swing. Confirm whether the porch is counted within the 20 × 30 or 20 × 32-foot external dimensions or is an additional projection.

9. Windows, Doors and Natural Ventilation

The concept shows windows on several exterior walls. The final architectural drawing should provide a window schedule with width, height, sill level and opening type. Consider daylight, privacy, security, rain exposure and cross-ventilation. Door widths and swings should be shown clearly, especially where bedroom, bathroom and kitchen doors meet a shared circulation area.

10. Exterior Style and Roof

The exterior reference uses a red tiled hipped roof with generous eaves, light-coloured walls, dark window frames and stone-look porch details. This gives the compact house a warm, traditional-modern appearance. Eaves can help shade walls and openings, but their dimensions and orientation should be selected for the site and climate.

The image does not include a roof plan, structural framing details, truss or rafter sizes, bracing, connections, pitch, gutter sizing or rainwater downpipe locations. These details must be designed for the actual building spans, roofing material, wind exposure and local regulations. The front, side and rear images are visual references, not measured elevations.

11. Interior Finishes and Furniture

The interior reference suggests a neutral palette, warm lighting, simple furniture and easy-to-clean floor finishes. Similar finishes can make a small home feel calm and bright. Choose furniture only after the room dimensions and circulation routes have been confirmed. Use wall-mounted or compact storage where it improves floor space without blocking windows or ventilation.

12. Electrical and Water-Service Planning

Plan lighting, switches and socket outlets before plastering. Include practical outlets for the refrigerator, kitchen appliances, television, internet equipment and bedside use. Electrical circuits, earthing and protective devices should be designed and installed by a qualified electrician under applicable local standards.

Coordinate the water supply, hot-water provision if needed, soil and waste pipes, inspection points, rainwater drainage and access for maintenance. Test concealed plumbing and bathroom waterproofing before closing walls or laying final finishes.

13. Suggested Construction Sequence

  1. Survey the land, levels, boundaries, access and local setback requirements.
  2. Resolve the 20 × 30 versus 20 × 32-foot discrepancy and redraw the floor plan to scale.
  3. Confirm room dimensions, wall thicknesses, doors, windows, furniture clearances and service routes.
  4. Obtain foundation and structural design advice and required approvals.
  5. Set out and construct the approved foundation and floor base.
  6. Build walls and structural elements to the approved drawings.
  7. Install the designed roof frame, covering, gutters and downpipes.
  8. Complete first-fix electrical, water and drainage services.
  9. Carry out plastering, waterproofing, ceilings and floor preparation.
  10. Install windows, doors, kitchen fittings, bathroom fixtures and electrical accessories.
  11. Complete painting, external drainage, paths and landscaping, then inspect and test the house.

14. Materials and Cost Guide

A dependable construction cost cannot be calculated from this illustration. The footprint itself is inconsistent, and the price will depend on the corrected floor area, foundation conditions, structural system, roofing material, wall construction, finish level, labour and transport. Obtain current local quotations after a measured drawing and written material specification are ready.

  • Site preparation, excavation and foundation
  • Concrete, reinforcement and structural components
  • Blocks or bricks, mortar, plaster and paint
  • Roof framing, tiles or other covering, gutters and downpipes
  • Doors, windows, glazing and hardware
  • Electrical wiring, distribution board, lighting and fittings
  • Water supply, drainage, waterproofing and bathroom fixtures
  • Kitchen cabinets, worktop and appliances
  • Flooring, ceilings, labour, transport, approvals and contingency

Ask contractors to quote against the same approved drawings and specifications. Record exclusions such as boundary walls, utility connections, landscaping and professional fees, and keep a separate contingency allowance.

15. Is This a Suitable Starting Concept?

The image communicates a compact two-bedroom home with a separate kitchen, one bathroom, a small porch and a familiar tiled-roof exterior. It can be useful for discussing appearance and broad room requirements. Its critical limitation is the mismatch between the overall dimensions and the labelled room lengths. The plan should be corrected and independently checked before it is used for approvals, estimating or construction.

Professional check before building: Ask a qualified local house designer or architect to prepare a coordinated, scaled plan showing the correct outside dimensions, internal room dimensions, wall thicknesses, door swings, window schedule, furniture clearances, roof plan, elevations, foundation and service routes. Confirm local planning and approval requirements for the site.

Conclusion

This 20 × 30-foot two-bedroom small-house concept offers a clear visual direction for a compact family home, but the dimension labels need correction. Confirm whether the intended footprint is 20 × 30 or 20 × 32 feet, then adjust the room arrangement so every space fits with realistic wall thicknesses and circulation. With those checks completed by a qualified professional, the concept can become a more reliable starting point for a site-specific home design.
