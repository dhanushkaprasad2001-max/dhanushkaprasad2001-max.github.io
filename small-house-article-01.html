20 × 36-Foot Modern Two-Bedroom Small House Plan

2D Floor Plan, Furnished 3D Layout, Exterior Design and Complete Planning Guide

A comfortable home does not have to be large. This 20 × 36-foot concept explores how a compact single-storey home can accommodate two bedrooms, a living room, a kitchen, a bathroom and a small front porch. The guide explains the proposed spaces and the decisions to review before turning an inspiration concept into a buildable design.

Important: This is an illustrative planning concept, not a certified architectural or structural drawing. The actual layout, room sizes, wall thicknesses, setbacks and structure must be checked by a qualified local professional.

1. House Design Overview

The proposed footprint is approximately 20 feet wide and 36 feet long, giving a simple rectangular starting point for a compact family home. Shared spaces are planned close to the entrance, while bedrooms are placed in a quieter part of the house. The arrangement should be adapted to the real site, its orientation and local building requirements.

2. Proposed 2D Floor Plan and Room Dimensions

The following room sizes are planning targets. They do not add up to a verified net internal area because wall thickness, circulation, service spaces and the exact porch arrangement still need to be resolved in a measured drawing.

Keep the main circulation route clear from the entrance to the living area, kitchen and bedroom zone. Check that door swings do not clash with furniture or each other. A professional drawing should show all external and internal dimensions, wall thicknesses and opening positions.

3. Bedroom 1 – Main Bedroom

Plan for a double bed with enough clearance to get in and out comfortably. Where the room permits, place small bedside tables on both sides and position a wardrobe along a wall that does not block the window or door. A dressing mirror can be fixed to a wardrobe door or placed in a compact corner.

4. Bedroom 2 – Flexible Family Room

The second bedroom can serve as a child's room, guest room or study-bedroom. Choose a bed size based on the occupants and retain a clear route to the wardrobe and window. A compact desk or wall-mounted shelf can add useful workspace without overcrowding the room.

5. Living Room Furniture Layout

A compact three-seat sofa or small L-shaped sofa can face a television unit. Use a modest coffee table and avoid oversized furniture. Keep a continuous walking route between the front entrance and the rest of the home. Window placement should support daylight while reducing glare on the television.

6. Kitchen Design

An L-shaped or straight counter may work in an 8 × 10-foot kitchen, depending on door and window positions. Organise the sink, preparation counter and cooking area so that the work sequence feels natural. Allow a suitable location for the refrigerator and storage. Confirm appliance clearances and ventilation before finalising cabinetry.

7. Bathroom Layout

Arrange the toilet, washbasin and shower so the door can open freely and the wet area is easy to clean. Provide suitable waterproofing, floor falls and drainage. Use an operable window or a properly designed mechanical exhaust system for ventilation.

8. Doors, Windows, Daylight and Ventilation

  • Place the main entrance from the covered front porch into the living area.
  • Position bedroom doors to preserve privacy and usable wall space.
  • Provide bedroom windows sized and located for daylight, privacy and cross-ventilation where possible.
  • Use living-room openings to bring in daylight while considering sun and rain exposure.
  • Provide kitchen ventilation near the cooking zone and suitable bathroom ventilation.

Exact window and door sizes depend on local standards, structural design, security, orientation and the actual site. Show every opening on the final drawings.

9. Furnished 3D Layout – What to Check

A furnished 3D top view is useful for understanding how the rooms connect and whether beds, wardrobes, sofas and kitchen units fit. Review furniture clearances, door swings, circulation and access to windows. The 3D view should be generated from the same dimensioned plan so that both representations agree; do not treat a decorative render as a measured drawing.

10. Modern Exterior and Roof Design

A simple contemporary exterior can use a clean rectangular form, a sheltered porch, appropriately sized windows and a restrained palette such as warm white, light grey and natural timber tones. Low-maintenance planting can soften the entrance without taking up too much space.

A sloping metal-sheet roof or another locally suitable roof system may be considered. Select the roof structure, covering, insulation, gutters and rainwater outlets according to wind exposure, rainfall, maintenance needs and budget. A qualified professional must size and detail the roof structure.

11. Electrical and Plumbing Planning

Plan lighting, switches and power outlets before plastering. Consider bedside outlets, television and internet points, kitchen appliance outlets, refrigerator supply, bathroom lighting and exterior security lights. Electrical work must be designed and installed to applicable local standards.

Keep kitchen and bathroom plumbing routes practical, with accessible shut-off points and maintainable drainage. Confirm water supply, waste-water disposal, ventilation and waterproofing details with the relevant professionals.

12. Main Construction Stages

  1. Survey the site, confirm boundaries and check local planning requirements.
  2. Assess soil and prepare the foundation design.
  3. Set out the building and construct the approved foundation.
  4. Complete ground-floor preparation and structural work.
  5. Build walls and install required lintels and structural elements.
  6. Install the designed roof frame, covering and rainwater system.
  7. Complete first-fix electrical and plumbing work.
  8. Apply plaster, waterproofing and required protective finishes.
  9. Install doors, windows, floors, ceilings and fittings.
  10. Complete painting, external works, testing and final inspection.

13. Budget and Materials Checklist

A reliable construction estimate needs a location, current labour rates, material specifications, soil information and a confirmed drawing. Do not rely on a generic price per square foot without checking what it includes.

  • Site preparation and foundation
  • Concrete, reinforcement and structural work
  • Blocks or bricks, mortar and plaster
  • Roof frame, covering, insulation and gutters
  • Doors, windows and hardware
  • Electrical wiring, fittings and testing
  • Water supply, drainage and bathroom fittings
  • Kitchen cabinets, worktops and appliances
  • Floor finishes, paint and ceiling finishes
  • Labour, transport, approvals and contingency

Collect current written quotations from local suppliers and contractors, then compare equivalent specifications. Keep a contingency allowance for site-specific work and price changes.

14. Is This Plan Suitable for Your Land?

This concept may be a useful starting point for a small family that needs two bedrooms and practical shared spaces. Before adopting it, confirm that the land can accommodate the building together with required setbacks, access, drainage, parking and outdoor service areas. Check local authority rules and have the final architectural, structural, electrical and plumbing documents prepared or verified by qualified professionals.

Conclusion

A compact home can feel comfortable when its layout is organised around everyday use. This 20 × 36-foot two-bedroom concept offers a starting point for planning the living room, bedrooms, kitchen, bathroom and entrance as one coordinated design. The next step is to adapt the concept to the actual site and produce accurate professional drawings before construction.
